# Art bible

The look: crisp, deliberate VGA-era pixels; strong value contrast; warm-shifted darkness; sandstone
and gold giving way to steel and cyan over the episode. One palette, one pixel scale, one light
direction.

## Palette plan (256 entries)

Source `assets/src/palette/catlantis.gpl` (format in `tech.md`). Every ramp is ordered **dark to
light** (lowest index darkest). Entry names are `<ramp>_<nn>` with nn from 00. Ramps are smooth in
Oklab lightness (roughly even L steps), hue drifting slightly warmer toward the dark end so shading
looks painted, not grey-dimmed.

reserved block through a palette PR approved by the art director; existing entries never change
once M1 lands.

## Colormap (32 rows)

Generated by `tools/colormap.py` into `assets/gen/colormap.bin`:
1. For row `r` in 0..31: `f = (32 - r) / 32`, `t = r / 31`.
2. Target (sRGB, 0..255 floats) = `(R*f, G*f*(1 - 0.10*t), B*f*(1 - 0.25*t))`: darker rows lose
   blue first, then green, so darkness is warm brown, not cold gray.
3. Convert target to Oklab, choose the nearest palette entry by Oklab Euclidean distance among
   entries that are not reserved, not fullbright and not transparent (ties: lowest index).
4. Row 0 is identity. Fullbright entries and 255 map to themselves in every row.
5. Same-ramp exception: entries of the `blue` ramp choose only among `blue_*`, `shadow_*` and
   `black` (same target, same distance, same tie rule). Otherwise light blue lands on the
   near-neutral `steel` ramp from row ~14 and a Blue Ankh in shade reads grey (issue #109). Check:
   `blue_07..blue_11` keep Oklab chroma >= 0.04 and b < 0 through row 20 (`test_colormap`).

The `shadow` entries exist so the darkest rows fade to black through warm brown. Without them
the near-black gap (black at Oklab L 0, ramp dark ends at L ~0.14-0.17) makes rows ~20-29 of the
near-neutral and cool ramps land on `indigo_00`, a navy, before black. Any later dark ramp must keep
this: no row of a non-cool ramp (everything except `blue`, `violet`, `indigo`) may map to a
blue-hued entry. Assets do not paint with `shadow_*` (too dark to read); palcheck allows them
because the colormap produces them.

All ramps except `fb_*` and `transparent` are warm-shifted by this rule. Acceptance check: row 16 of
`steel_12` must land in a warmer hue (higher Oklab b) than the plain scaled colour would.

UI dims (menu backdrops, pause) are not lighting and do not keep fullbright bright. A screen
darkened behind menu text through colormap row `r` remaps a fullbright entry `i` (240-254) to
`row r` of `lit(i)`. Here `lit(i)` is the nearest palette entry to `i` by Oklab, among entries that
are not reserved, not fullbright and not transparent (ties: lowest index, the same rule as step 3).
Every other index uses row `r` unchanged. Without this rule, glows, key shimmers and pickup
crests stay at full brightness behind the menu and break up the gold menu text
(PR #136, pause over pickups).

## Light and shading rules

- **One light direction for everything**: key light from the **top-left, 45 degrees**, in front of
  the viewer. Textures: highlights on top and left edges of bevels, shadows bottom-right. Sprites:
  pre-rendered with a sun lamp from upper-left-front (Blender: rotation X=45, Z=-45 relative to the
  camera, so it rotates with the camera for all 8 directions), plus a weak fill (20%) along the
  camera axis, from the viewer (Blender: rotation X=90, Z=0 relative to the camera), so it reaches
  every face the camera sees and can never rim-light. Never rim lights.
- **Outline rule**: world sprites (enemies, pickups, props) get a 1 px **selective outline**: on
  silhouette edges facing away from the light (right and bottom) use the darkest entry of the
  local ramp; on lit edges no outline. Never pure black outlines, never outlines on wall or floor
  textures. HUD art and fonts use a 1 px dark shadow bottom-right instead.
- **Dithering rule**: no error diffusion anywhere; the quantizer is pure Oklab nearest. Hand
  dithering is allowed only as a 2x2 checker between two **adjacent** entries of the same ramp, on
  textures and HUD panels, never on sprites, never on small (<16 px) detail.
- **Contrast**: every enemy sprite's dominant ramp spans at least 8 entries, and its silhouette
  must read when filled with a single colour (the art director checks the silhouette at 1x).
- Colour budget per sprite: at most 3 ramps plus fullbright accents.

Pre-rendered enemies use an orthographic camera at eye height, fixed pixel scale (64 px per Blender
unit), and are rendered at exactly the final size (no downscaling from large renders that turns
into mush); compositing adds edge contrast before quantization.

Pre-render exposure and ramps (rules for `tools/blender_render.py`):
- **Exposure**: a face square to the key light renders at its material's base colour (Cycles sun
  strength `pi`, fill `0.2 * pi`) with colour management view transform `Standard`, look `None`,
  exposure 0, gamma 1 (never AgX or Filmic: AgX, Blender's default, darkens and desaturates lit
  faces by about 16 sRGB levels before quantization). Every material must reach the upper half of
  its ramp on lit faces; a sprite whose brightest pixel stops mid-ramp fails the 8-entry contrast
  rule above.
- **No black holes**: every face the camera sees gets some fill, so no opaque pixel inside the
  silhouette renders as `black`, `shadow_*` or a ramp's `_00` (`_00` is reserved for the outline).
  A fill placement that leaves faces edge-on to it while in key shadow does not meet this.
- **Ramp-constrained quantization**: `sprite.json` maps each material to one ramp (at most 3 ramps
  plus fullbright per sprite). Each pixel is quantized to the Oklab-nearest entry **within its
  material's ramp**; the outline uses that ramp's `_00`. Fullbright materials map to their own
  `fb_*` entry and never into a ramp. Global nearest-over-the-palette lets dark tones of one
  material jump to a neighbouring ramp (gold to olive, gray to steel), which breaks the colour
  budget. This is still pure nearest, with no dithering.

## View models (first-person weapons)

Hand-drawn in screen pixels, bottom-centred over the 320x208 view (sizes in `weapons.md`). They
follow the HUD light rule (top-left key, 1 px dark shadow bottom-right, no outline) plus:

- **Aim**: the barrel axis, extended on screen, crosses the horizon row (y=104) within 160+-24 px.
  A gun pointing well off the centre reads as swung, not aimed.
- **Proportion**: a barrel or slide is clearly longer than it is wide on screen (length at least
  2x the rear face width), with a dark bore (2-3 px of a ramp's darkest entries) at the muzzle.
- **Paws** are drawn like the claw view model (`vm1`): separate toe lobes split by 1 px dark fur,
  a knuckle highlight, and fingers that overlap and wrap the grip. A grip never stands on top of
  the paw like a pedestal, and a paw is never a shaded circle.
- **Forearms** are shaded as cylinders (light on the left edge, dark on the right) with 3-5
  irregular, curved tabby bands across the arm axis. Straight, even diagonal hatching reads as wood
  and is rejected, because it merges with wooden grips and stocks.
- All frames of all weapons share one paw and arm treatment.

## Rejected looks (the art director rejects on sight)

- Blur, bilinear scaling, anti-aliased edges against transparency, "AA mush" from downscaled renders.
- Off-palette pixels, error-diffusion noise, banded photo gradients, airbrushed soft shading.
- Pillow-shading (light from the centre outward) and inconsistent light direction.
- Generic placeholders: flat coloured boxes, programmer art, text labels instead of art, clip-art.
- Mixed pixel scales: a sprite whose pixels are visibly bigger or smaller than the wall texels.
- Pure black outlines; neon colours outside the fullbright entries; colour cycling as a crutch.
- Anything recognisable from another game's characters, enemies, HUD or textures.
